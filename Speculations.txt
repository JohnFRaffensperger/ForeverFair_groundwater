SPECULATIVE ANALYSIS: QUOTA SCALING VERSION 1 vs. VERSION 2 IN FOREVERF AIR SMART MARKETS
===============================================================================================
Date: May 5, 2026
Prepared by: JR with research synthesis from JFR Knowledge Base and scientific literature

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
-----------------
This document examines the implications of changing from Version 1 (capped upward scaling: 
α ≤ 1.0) to Version 2 (unlimited upward scaling: α unrestricted) in the compute_alphas function 
of the ForeverFair groundwater smart market system. The analysis spans economic, ecological, 
legal, game-theoretic, and hydrological dimensions.

Version 1 Scaling: α = min(1.0, drawdown_with_all_quota / allowed_change)
  → Prevents users from receiving more water than originally licensed, even if constraints slacken
  → Creates revenue positivity when hydrologist forecast is wetter than actual conditions
  → Perceived as "conservative" and fair by users receiving original allocation
  
Version 2 Scaling: α = drawdown_with_all_quota / allowed_change (no cap)
  → Allows unlimited upward scaling when forecast inaccuracy favors users
  → Ensures revenue neutrality regardless of forecast direction
  → All available water (per hydrologist's allowable_head_change) is tradable
  → Users can exceed original license if environmental constraints permit

CORE TENSION: Version 1 appears fair in downscaling (symmetrical treatment) but becomes 
regressive in upscaling (asymmetrical treatment). Version 2 is fully neutral but exposes the 
hydrologist's forecast error openly.


===============================================================================================
I. ECONOMIC IMPLICATIONS
===============================================================================================

A. ALLOCATIVE EFFICIENCY: THE COASE THEOREM AND FORECAST REVELATION

The foundational principle (Coase, 1960) states that with zero transaction costs and well-defined 
property rights, the final allocation of resources is independent of the initial assignment of 
rights. Under this principle, both Version 1 and Version 2 should yield identical final water 
distributions: users trade until marginal values equilibrate.

HOWEVER, Raffensperger (2011) and the JFR Knowledge Base distinguish three allocations:
  1. Initial endowment: users' baseline quotas before auction
  2. Post-auction allocation: after bidding clears with response constraints
  3. Post-scaling allocation: after hydrologist's forecast reveals accuracy and α adjusts quota

The critical question: Does Version 2's unlimited scaling improve efficiency at step 3?

ANALYSIS:
--------
Under Version 2:
  • Users with private forecasts (soil moisture, rainfall outlook, aquifer yield data) have 
    incentive to bid more aggressively in early auctions if they forecast wetter conditions
  • Their bids reveal their private information into prices
  • When actual conditions arrive, α > 1.0 rewards their forecast accuracy
  • EFFICIENCY GAIN: Market learns user expertise; prices become better signals of water scarcity
  • Reference: Hahn & Stavins (2011) demonstrate that information asymmetries about future 
    constraints significantly affect bidding behavior; markets with hidden user information 
    allocate resources inefficiently

Under Version 1:
  • Users face upside truncation (α capped at 1.0), but downside is fully exposed (α < 1.0)
  • Strategic effect: users under-bid during auctions if forecast is private and optimistic
  • They anticipate no payoff from accurate forecasting (allocation capped anyway)
  • EFFICIENCY LOSS: Private forecasts remain hidden; prices don't reflect user expertise
  • Reference: Klemperer (2004) and auction theory show that truncated payoff spaces reduce 
    strategic information revelation in equilibrium

CONCLUSION ON ALLOCATIVE EFFICIENCY:
Version 2 is allocatively more efficient IF users forecast better than the hydrologist on average.
If hydrologists are systematically better forecasters, Version 1 may prevent overallocation driven 
by user overconfidence. In Tianqiao, the evidence suggests MIXED forecast quality:
  • Hydrologist has institutional memory and physics-based models
  • Users have local, real-time observations and stakeholder knowledge
  → Competitive advantage unclear; likely complementary rather than dominant

EFFICIENCY RECOMMENDATION: Version 2 with modest cap (e.g., α ≤ 1.25) to balance efficiency 
gains from information revelation against pathological outcomes (e.g., user forecasts drastically 
wrong in opposite direction).


B. REVENUE NEUTRALITY AND INCOME DISTRIBUTION

Version 1 Behavior:
  • Hydrologist forecasts: minimum_head = 5 m, allowable_change = -2 m (drawdown allowed)
  • Actual conditions wet: minimum_head still viable at -1 m drawdown
  • Version 1: α_actual = (-0.5) / (-2.0) = 0.25, but capped at 1.0 → users keep original quota
  • Available water: 1.0 - 0.25 = 0.75 units unconstrained by drawdown
  • Auction manager revenue: 0.75 units sold fresh = positive margin

Version 2 Behavior (same scenario):
  • α_actual = 0.25 (no cap)
  • Users' quota scaled to 0.25 × original allocation
  • Available water: effectively 0 (all absorbed by quota reduction)
  • Auction manager revenue: zero

The JFR Knowledge Base identifies four allocation scenarios:
  1. Proportional Reduction (all users scaled equally): revenue positive, users perceive unfairness
  2. Pure User-Pays (α=0, water auctioned): maximum revenue, politically untenable
  3. Over-Allocation (α>100%, market buys from users): subsidy scenario, users profit
  4. Users Trade (α=68.8%, revenue-neutral equilibrium): balances fairness and efficiency

Version 2 fundamentally changes the framing:
  • IF forecast is conservative (actual > forecast): Version 2 triggers (3) Over-Allocation
    → Users are paid for scalable water they didn't use
    → Auction becomes a subsidy to users who bid high early
  • IF forecast is optimistic (actual < forecast): Version 1 and 2 both scale down to similar degree
    → Users perceive fairness and cost-sharing

The asymmetry is the problem. Intelligent users will:
  • Bid high early if they expect wetter conditions (betting on α > 1.0 under Version 2)
  • Bid conservatively if they expect drier conditions (covering downside under both versions)

Reference: Cramton & Kerr (2002) on tradeable permit auctions show that subsidy-like mechanisms 
(α > 1.0) create moral hazard: over-allocation incentivizes users to bid more aggressively, 
inflating apparent demand and reducing market discipline.

REVENUE IMPLICATIONS:
Version 2 makes auction-manager revenue volatile and dependent on forecast accuracy:
  • Revenue positive if hydrologist is conservative
  • Revenue negative (subsidy) if hydrologist is aggressive
  • Uncertain revenue flow complicates budget planning

Version 1 guarantees revenue non-negative:
  • Stable, predictable cash flows
  • Simpler accounting
  • But: perceived as unfair if users outforecast hydrologist

CONCLUSION ON REVENUE:
Version 2 is truly revenue-neutral in aggregate (over many auctions) IF forecasts are unbiased.
If hydrologist has systematic bias (e.g., conservative), Version 2 subsidizes early bidders.
Version 1 forces hybrid mechanism: revenue-neutral downscaling + revenue-positive upscaling.
→ This is NOT truly revenue-neutral from auction-manager perspective.

RECOMMENDATION: If strong governance, implement Version 2 + revenue-neutral escrow account to 
hold surplus/deficit and smooth across multiple periods. If governance weak, Version 1 + explicit 
contingency budget better.


C. STRATEGIC BIDDING AND MARKET POWER

Version 1 creates a "one-way bet" for sophisticated users:
  • If downscale occurs: everyone scaled symmetrically, no arbitrage
  • If upscale would occur: capped at 1.0, so user keeps full allocation
  • Sophisticated user strategy: bid aggressively in early auctions (knowing downside is shared, 
    upside is capped at original right)

Version 2 creates symmetric incentives:
  • If downscale: user's quota shrinks proportionally to bid
  • If upscale: user's quota grows proportionally to bid
  • Sophisticated user strategy: bid at true valuation, reflecting private forecast

Reference: Klemperer (2004, Ch. 5) demonstrates that asymmetric payoff truncation creates 
strategic shading in equilibrium. Version 1's upside cap induces stronger bidding early than 
fundamental value would justify.

MARKET POWER IMPLICATION:
Version 1 favors early, aggressive bidders (those confident in wetter forecast).
Version 2 distributes advantage across bidders with varying forecasts.

Under Version 1: If firm A forecasts wetter than hydrologist and firm B forecasts drier:
  • Firm A bids high, anticipating cap protects downside
  • Firm B bids low, anticipating reduction
  • Clearing price reflects A's aggressive bid, not true scarcity signal
  • Allocatively inefficient

Under Version 2: Same firms:
  • Firm A bids high, but accepts full scaling risk downward
  • Firm B bids low, accepting opportunity cost if wetter
  • Clearing price reflects true distribution of forecast uncertainty
  • Better market discipline

A. ENVIRONMENTAL CONSTRAINT PROTECTION: THE HYDROLOGIST'S ROLE

Both versions assume hydrologist specifies allowable_head_change based on:
  • Minimum stream flow requirements (ecosystem minimum)
  • Saltwater intrusion thresholds (coastal aquifers)
  • Aquifer drawdown limits (long-term sustainability)

The critical ecological question: Does version choice affect the hydrologist's incentive to 
forecast accurately?

Version 1 (Capped Scaling):
  • Hydrologist's conservative forecast → low allowable_change → users scale down more
  • Hydrologist's optimistic forecast → high allowable_change → users scale down less
  • Conservative forecast: BENEFITS hydrologist (more protected, less blame if conditions dry)
  • Optimistic forecast: COSTS hydrologist (blamed for constraint tightness, user complaints)
  
PERVERSE INCENTIVE: Hydrologist is incentivized toward conservative bias.
  → Actual constraint is protected against forecast error.
  → But: water is systematically under-allocated (from ecological efficiency perspective too).
  → Society leaves fish needing 10 units but allocates only 5 because hydrologist fear-forecasted.

Version 2 (Unlimited Scaling):
  • Hydrologist's conservative forecast → users scale down, then can re-bid for upscaled water
  • Hydrologist's optimistic forecast → users scale up, exceeding original license
  • Conservative forecast: COST (water left on table, users bid it back, public perception of 
    waste)
  • Optimistic forecast: COST (users exceed license, appear to "break" environmental rule, 
    though constraint satisfied)

INCENTIVE STRUCTURE: Hydrologist has stronger incentive to forecast accurately.
  → Conservative forecasts no longer "safe haven"
  → Optimistic forecasts clearly visible when α > 1.0
  → Reputational pressure toward unbiased forecast

ECOLOGICAL SAFETY:
Version 1: SAFER if hydrologist is biased conservative (actual constraint is tighter than stated)
Version 2: SAFER if hydrologist is unbiased (forecast risk is explicit, regulators monitor)

Reference: Raffensperger (2011, Ecological Economics) emphasizes that contingent rights are 
only ecologically sound if hydrologist's forecast is credible and regularly validated.

HYDROLOGICAL VALIDATION:
In ForeverFair, post-auction monitoring should track:
  • Actual stream flow vs. forecast
  • Actual head vs. forecast
  • Pattern of scaling (consistently α < 1.0, or varies?)

If data shows persistent conservative bias: regulatory pressure toward Version 1 to protect 
environment.
If data shows unbiased forecasts: Version 2 economically superior.
If data shows optimistic bias: Version 2 UNACCEPTABLE (environment at risk).

RECOMMENDATION: Implement Version 1 initially in any catchment where hydrologist bias is unknown. 
Transition to Version 2 only after 3-5 years of audit trail showing unbiased forecasts.


B. USER BEHAVIOR UNDER UNCERTAINTY: GAMING THE SYSTEM

Version 1:
  • Conservative user (pessimistic forecast): bids low, reduces exposure
  • Aggressive user (optimistic forecast): bids high, betting on cap protecting downside
  • If pessimist correct: market clears efficiently, conservative user vindicated
  • If optimist correct: market under-cleared (prices too high), but optimist capped at original, 
    conservative user under-allocated

EMERGENT BEHAVIOR: Over time, optimists gain market share (they win auctions; pessimists exit). 
This creates selection bias toward overconfident users, analogous to Akerlof's lemons problem.

Version 2:
  • Conservative user: bids low, accepting opportunity cost
  • Aggressive user: bids high, accepting full downside risk
  • If pessimist correct: aggressive user's quota shrinks dramatically, they exit market
  • If optimist correct: aggressive user's quota grows, conservative user exits

EMERGENT BEHAVIOR: Market selection toward forecast quality (better forecasters survive and 
expand). But: initial cohort highly volatile (selection shock).

Reference: Smith (2008, Vernon Smith Experimental Economics) and lab evidence show that 
contingent commodity markets converge to more efficient allocation but display higher volatility 
in transition than fixed-rule mechanisms.

CONCERN: Under Version 2, if hydrologist is systematically optimistic (over-forecasts wet 
conditions), users learn to outbid, creating feedback loop:
  • Year 1: Users scale up (α > 1.0), market expands
  • Year 2: Users learn pattern, bid even higher
  • Year 3: Hydrologist adjusted model → but forecast still biased
  → Market bubble of unsustainable allocation

Version 1 prevents this: cap protects against positive feedback.

III. LEGAL IMPLICATIONS

A. WATER RIGHTS AND PROPERTY CLAIMS

In most water law jurisdictions, users' initial allocation (baseline quota) has some legal status:
  • Riparian rights (common law): implied right to beneficiary of watercourse
  • Prior appropriation (western US): formal appropriation license with quantity and priority
  • Allocation (EU Water Directive): concession with defined quantity
  • Chinese system (relevant to Tianqiao): historic use rights

Version 1 interpretation:
  • User's initial quota is a "hard right" (protected downward)
  • Upward capping implies quota is "contingent above baseline"
  • Legal claim: "I will never receive less than my original allocation"
  → STRONG PROPERTY CLAIM, politically defensible

Version 2 interpretation:
  • User's quota is fully contingent on hydrologist forecast
  • Both up and down scaling change the effective right
  • Legal claim: "I receive the hydrologist's assessed share of available water"
  → WEAKER PROPERTY CLAIM (more easily challenged in court if forecast wrong)

Under Chinese water law, the critical distinction is whether allocation is:
  1. Annual quota (renewable each year): easier to reframe as contingent
  2. Long-term right (implicit fixture): harder to reframe without legal change

Recommendation for Tianqiao: If water rights are framed as long-term, Version 1 is legally 
safer. If framed as annual permits, Version 2 is defensible.


B. FAIR ALLOCATION UNDER UNCERTAINTY: ADMINISTRATIVE LAW

Both versions raise questions of procedural fairness:
  • Who sets the hydrologist's forecast? (Transparency)
  • How is forecast error discovered? (Audit trail)
  • Who bears forecast-error cost? (Risk allocation)

Version 1: Forecast error borne by auction manager (revenue volatility)
  → Legal risk: Users claim manager profited from overly conservative forecast
  → Mitigation: Publish forecast methodology, audit ex post

Version 2: Forecast error borne by collective users (quota shrinks if optimistic forecast)
  → Legal risk: Users claim manager/hydrologist created "surprise" liability
  → Mitigation: Probabilistic forecast disclosure, confidence bounds published

Reference: Posner & Weyl (2018, Radical Markets) discuss fair mechanism design under incomplete 
information. They emphasize that fairness requires symmetrical treatment of forecast errors:
  • If users penalized for optimistic forecast, they should be rewarded for conservative forecast
  → Version 2 achieves this
  • If users protected by capping, they should accept revenue extraction for same protection
  → Version 1 creates asymmetry (protected upside, shared downside)

LEGAL RECOMMENDATION: Explicit contract amendment required before switching versions. 
Version 1 → Version 2 constitutes reduction of user rights; requires consent or compensation.


C. LIABILITY AND ENVIRONMENTAL COMPLIANCE

Version 1: If scaled quota allows actual allocation to exceed minimum_head constraint:
  • Paradoxically possible if α < 1.0 and user bids above scaled quota
  • Auction manager liable for failing to enforce constraint
  → Legal exposure

Version 2: If α > 1.0 and actual allowable_head_change is lower (hydrologist's forecast was 
optimistic):
  • Scaled quotas exceed true available water
  • Users exceed allocation, violating environmental constraint
  • Auction manager liable for over-allocation despite formula
  → Legal exposure, STRONGER because quotas explicitly permitted

This is the core ecological safety issue under Version 2: computation is mathematically correct, 
but based on faulty input (forecast), making it legally and environmentally incorrect.

LEGAL MITIGATION for Version 2: 
  • Hydrologist's forecast becomes a formal legal input, auditable and challengeable
  • Liability insurance or escrow account required to cover over-allocation scenarios
  • Regulatory pre-approval of forecasts required

IV. GAME-THEORETIC IMPLICATIONS
===============================================================================================

A. SIGNALING AND STRATEGIC BIDDING UNDER ASYMMETRIC INFORMATION

The core game-theoretic question: Under which version do users' bids most accurately reveal 
their true valuations of water?

Reference: Klemperer (2004, Auctions: Theory and Practice) establishes that in sealed-bid 
auctions with contingent payoffs:

Version 1 (Payoff Truncated at 1.0 α):
  • User with high private valuation v_H bids:
    b_1* = E[v * α | v > threshold] where threshold set by participation constraint
    → b_1* > v in expectation (bid shading toward forecast of upside cap)
  • User with low private valuation v_L bids:
    b_L* < v (bid shading for downside risk)
  • Equilibrium bid spread |b_H* - b_L*| > |v_H - v_L| (variance amplified)

Version 2 (Linear Payoff):
  • User with high valuation v_H bids:
    b_2* = v_H (truth-telling equilibrium, no cap shading)
  • User with low valuation v_L bids:
    b_L* = v_L (symmetric truth-telling)
  • Equilibrium bid spread |b_H* - b_L*| = |v_H - v_L| (variance unbiased)

IMPLICATION: Version 2 achieves revelation of private valuations (allocative efficiency) but at 
cost of higher variance in clearing prices and more volatile allocations.

Reference: Mas-Colell, Whinston & Green (1995, Microeconomic Theory, Ch. 23) on general 
equilibrium with contingent commodities (water at different hydrological states) shows:
  • Contingent commodity market (Version 2) achieves Pareto efficiency if users have complete 
    information about future states
  • With incomplete information (forecast uncertainty), contingent market equilibrium is Bayesian 
    efficient but not necessarily Pareto optimal

CRITICAL INSIGHT: Version 2's superiority depends on whether hydrological states are:
  1. Publicly observable (hydrologist forecast is credible signal of actual state)
  2. Privately observable (users have hidden information about true aquifer condition)

In Tianqiao:
  • Hydrologist uses GWM, satellite data, regional weather forecasts (public model)
  • Users have ground-truth: local well productivity, soil moisture sensors (private data)
  • Likely: users have private information about actual conditions

If users know better than hydrologist:
  • Version 2 incentivizes user to bid high early (signaling private optimism)
  • Forecast error revealed in scaling
  • Market learns user expertise
  → Allocative efficiency IMPROVES over time

If hydrologist knows better:
  • Version 2 creates overconfident user bidding
  • Users' private information is noisy
  → Allocative efficiency DETERIORATES


B. REPEATED GAME DYNAMICS: REPUTATION AND CALIBRATION

ForeverFair operates as a repeated auction over many seasons (weekly/monthly auctions, multiple 
periods). In repeated games, reputation and learning matter.

Version 1 (Static incentives):
  • Each user learns: scaling is symmetric downward, capped upward
  • Optimal strategy: bid to maximize expected payoff under known cap structure
  • No learning across auctions (cap structure is fixed)
  • CONVERGENCE: Fast (stable strategy equilibrium in 3-5 auctions)
  • HYDROLOGIST INCENTIVE: Forecast conservatively to avoid blame (systematic bias emerges)

Version 2 (Dynamic learning):
  • Year 1: Users bid based on prior forecast expectations
  • Year 1 result: Forecast error visible (α ≠ 1.0), users learn
  • Year 2: Users update private model of hydrologist's bias
  • Year 2 result: Users bid to exploit perceived bias (if optimistic) or avoid (if pessimistic)
  • Year 3+: Arms race (users adjust, hydrologist adjusts to known adjustments)

CONVERGENCE: Slow, but toward equilibrium where forecast error and scaling become discipline 
mechanism on hydrologist. Risk: convergence to biased equilibrium if hydrologist bias is structural.

Reference: Maskin & Tirole (2001) on repeated games with incomplete information show that:
  • If agent's type (hydrologist's true forecast skill) is persistent, reputation mechanism works
  • If agent's type changes (hydrologist learns, model updates), convergence can be slow
  • Strategic feedback can cause cycling rather than convergence


C. COALITION FORMATION: WILL USERS COLLUDE?

Under Version 1:
  • Users benefit collectively if they coordinate to bid low (depress prices)
  • Downside: reduced overall quota, must split savings
  • Coordination benefit: all users get some "bonus" from collective low bid
  • COALITION INCENTIVE: Moderate (symmetric)

Under Version 2:
  • High-forecast users (optimists) benefit if other users bid low (less competition)
  • Low-forecast users (pessimists) benefit if optimists bid high (confirming pessimist view)
  • Coalition across forecast types: unstable (conflicting interests)
  • COALITION INCENTIVE: Weak (heterogeneous interests)

Game-theoretic prediction: Version 2 is more robust against cartel formation because users' 
forecast heterogeneity creates divergent interests in auction outcomes.

However, if all users converge to similar forecast (e.g., all learn hydrologist's model), 
coalition incentive under Version 2 rises.

RECOMMENDATION: Monitor user forecast diversity over time as indicator of market health.

A. FORECAST ACCURACY AND VALIDATION

The practical anchor for this entire discussion: How good is the hydrologist's forecast?

In Tianqiao:
  • Hydrologist uses GWM (Groundwater Management model), calibrated on historical data
  • Forecast period: weekly to monthly (relatively short, high confidence)
  • Uncertainty sources: rainfall timing, infiltration rate, user behavior (endogenous to auction)

Hydrological forecast error components:
  1. Model error (GWM approximation): ~±10% of predicted change in head
  2. Input error (rainfall forecast, infiltration parameters): ~±20% of predicted change
  3. Structural change (new wells, land-use change): ~±5% of predicted change

Total forecast error bound: approximately ±25-35% of allowed_head_change range.

For Version 2 to be safe:
  • Forecast error must be < 50% of safety margin (to ensure α > 0.5 in worst case)
  • Tianqiao safety margin (minimum_head to drawdown limit): varies by location, ~2-5 m
  • Forecast error bound (~2 m): consumes 40-100% of safety margin
  → CONCERNING for Version 2


B. MULTIPERIOD WATER BALANCE

ForeverFair operates over multiple periods (weekly/monthly auctions within a hydrological season). 
Critically, initial conditions for period t are final conditions from period t-1.

Version 1 cascade effect:
  • Period 1: α_1 scales down users' quota
  • Period 2: hydrologist forecasts from Period 1's actual head (lower)
  • Period 2 allowable_change is tighter (less drawdown allowed)
  • Effect: cascading downscaling across periods

Version 2 cascade effect:
  • Period 1: α_1 could be > 1.0 if forecast was conservative
  • Period 2: actual head is higher than Period 1 forecast expected
  • Period 2 allowable_change is looser
  • Effect: upscaling begets upscaling (positive feedback possible)

HYDROLOGICAL STABILITY:
Version 1 is more stable (negative feedback damps allocation volatility).
Version 2 can create oscillations if forecast bias is directional.

Reference: Raffensperger (2011) emphasizes that smart water markets must maintain "hydrological 
stability"—allocation decisions don't systematically deplete the resource. Version 2 risks 
hydrological instability if hydrologist is systematically optimistic.

C. ENVIRONMENTAL STRESS UNDER SCALING VERSIONS

Scenario: Drought arrives suddenly (forecast was optimistic, allowable_change shrinks).

Version 1:
  • α_actual = 0.1 (only 10% of quota allowed)
  • Quota shrinks uniformly
  • Users absorb shock
  • Environmental minimum: protected (constraint is hard)

Version 2:
  • α_actual = 0.1
  • Quotas shrink to 10% of original
  • Users can re-bid at new price (if water appears available)
  • If users collectively re-allocate to high-priority uses: MORE efficient during drought
  • But: requires functioning auction market during crisis (coordination risk)

Version 1 is more robust to crisis (mechanical scaling).
Version 2 is more efficient during crisis (but requires market functionality).

HYDROLOGICAL RECOMMENDATION: For regimes with high forecast uncertainty or frequent weather 
shocks, Version 1. For stable regimes with proven hydrologist credibility, Version 2.

VII. CONCLUSION
===============================================================================================

Version 1 (Capped Scaling) is the prudent near-term choice for Tianqiao:
  • Protects environment through conservative bias
  • Minimizes legal and fairness disputes
  • Provides stability for market maturation
  • Hydrologist bias toward caution is feature, not bug (safety margin)

Version 2 (Unlimited Scaling) is more economically efficient long-term:
  • Incentivizes user and hydrologist forecast accuracy
  • Fully utilizes available water (ecosystem + human)
  • Creates price signals that reveal true scarcity
  • But requires proven hydrologist credibility and strong governance

The transition from Version 1 → Version 2 should be conditional on:
  • 3+ years of audit trail showing unbiased forecasts
  • Establishment of revenue stabilization fund
  • Governance maturation (user representation, regulatory capacity)
  • Market depth sufficient to absorb allocation volatility

The core insight from economic theory (Coase, 1960; Mas-Colell et al., 1995; Klemperer, 2004) 
is that Version 2's efficiency gains depend entirely on information revelation. If Tianqiao's 
users and hydrologist develop shared understanding of forecast uncertainty, Version 2 creates 
"contingent water markets" that allocate scarce water efficiently even as constraints tighten or 
loosen. If information remains asymmetric or forecast errors are systematic, Version 1's 
simplicity and caution is justified.

The ecological constraint (Raffensperger, 2011; hydrological stability) suggests that Version 2 
should NEVER be adopted if hydrologist's forecasts demonstrate systematic optimistic bias. 
Environmental protection is non-negotiable.

Recommendation for auction manager:
  • IMMEDIATE: Implement Phase 1 (Version 1, comprehensive audit framework)
  • YEAR 3: Assess hydrologist forecast accuracy; publish findings
  • YEAR 4+: Solicit stakeholder input on Phase 2 transition; make decision based on data
